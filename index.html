About WC

The FIFA World Cup is the world's most prestigious international football tournament. Held every four years, it brings together the best national teams from around the globe to compete for the ultimate prize in football. Since its first tournament in 1930, the World Cup has become a celebration of passion, skill, teamwork, and national pride. Millions of fans follow the tournament, creating an exciting atmosphere both inside stadiums and across the world. Every edition produces unforgettable moments, from stunning goals and dramatic penalty shootouts to inspiring underdog stories and legendary performances by some of football's greatest . The FIFA World Cup is more than just a sporting event—it is a global celebration that unites people of different cultures, languages, and backgrounds through their shared love of the game. As new generations of players and fans continue to embrace the tournament, the World Cup remains a symbol of excellence, sportsmanship, and the enduring spirit of football.

Unexpected Events

## Unexpected & Funny World Cup Moments The FIFA World Cup has produced countless unforgettable moments, many of which had fans laughing or left the football world in complete disbelief. One of the most famous surprises came in 1950 when Uruguay defeated heavily favored Brazil in the final, a result so shocking it became known as the **"Maracanazo."** Football has also seen its share of funny incidents. In the 1990 World Cup, England midfielder Paul Gascoigne cried after receiving a yellow card because he thought it would keep him out of the final if England qualified. Although England never reached the final, his emotional reaction became one of football's most memorable images. Another unforgettable moment came in 1994 when Colombian defender Andrés Escobar accidentally scored an own goal against the United States. While heartbreaking rather than funny, it remains one of the tournament's most unexpected moments. In 2002, tournament favorites France were eliminated in the group stage without scoring a single goal, stunning football fans around the world. More recently, the 2022 World Cup delivered one of the biggest upsets in history when Saudi Arabia defeated eventual champions Argentina 2–1 in the opening group stage. These unexpected victories, emotional reactions, and unusual moments remind fans that the FIFA World Cup is more than just a competition—it is a stage where anything can happen, creating stories that are remembered for generations.

1958, 1962, 1970 - Pelé (Brazil)

Pelé remains the only player in history to win three FIFA World Cups. He burst onto the world stage as a 17-year-old in 1958, scoring twice in the final. His combination of skill, vision, and goal-scoring ability makes him arguably the most iconic player in the tournament's history.

1986 - Diego Maradona (Argentina)

Maradona delivered what is widely considered the greatest individual performance in World Cup history during the 1986 tournament in Mexico. He captained Argentina to glory, highlighted by his infamous "Hand of God" and breathtaking "Goal of the Century" against England.
